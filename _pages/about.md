I am a PhD candidate in Economics at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, with an expected completion date of 2027.

My dissertation, *Three Essays on Outsourcing Social Reproduction in India*, studies paid domestic work, the outsourced share of the daily work of maintaining households and caring for their members, and how it is distributed across gender, caste, and class. In my job market paper, I use India's national time-use surveys (TUS) to analyze both sides of paid domestic work, the households that hire domestic workers and the workers themselves. A second essay dives deeper into live-in domestic work, examining how the worker's gender shapes both the employing household's time and the reproductive labor the worker performs for it, using TUS. A third traces how less-educated urban women have been pushed into own-account and domestic work as wage employment has contracted between 2017-18 and 2023-24, using the Periodic Labour Force Survey data.

I did a Bachelor's in economics at Miranda House, University of Delhi, and a Master's in Economics at Ambedkar University Delhi. At the latter, my introduction to heterodox economics deepened my interest in the connections between gender, labor, and development — themes that continue to shape my research.

## Outsourcing Social Reproduction: Determinants and Consequences of Paid Domestic Work in Urban India

The paper examines outsourcing of the domestic component of social reproduction in urban India — defined as the daily work of maintaining, feeding, and caring for household members — which also reproduces the class and caste order that determines which households purchase it, who performs it, and how the arrangement reshapes the workday on both sides of the transaction. Using India’s 2019 and 2024 Time Use Surveys, I examine the consequences for both employing households and domestic workers, distinguishing live-in and live-out arrangements.
