Job Market Paper

Outsourcing Social Reproduction: Determinants and Consequences of Paid Domestic Work in Urban India

Hiring a paid domestic worker in urban India transfers part of the daily work of maintaining, feeding, and caring for household members across caste and class hierarchies. This domestic outsourcing has time consequences on both sides of the transaction, which have never been analyzed using a nationally representative dataset. This paper uses pooled data from the Pan-India Time Use Survey 2019 and 2024 to quantify the gendered impact of paid domestic labor for both employers and workers, separately for live-in and live-out arrangements. On the employer side, the gains accrue to women in households at the top of the caste and class order. Relative to non-outsourcing households, the presence of a live-in worker is associated with nearly twice the reduction in women’s unpaid time and nearly three times the increase in their paid time observed under live-out arrangements, while men’s time is largely unaffected. On the worker side, the length of the workday is similar for women and men within each arrangement, but among live-out workers, women spend nearly four times as much time on unpaid work as men. Taken together, this transaction places two women at its opposite ends, but their class and caste locations determine which one outsources it and which one takes it up.

Works in Progress

Social Reproduction on Call: The Gendered Economy of Live-in Domestic Work in India

Using India’s 2024 Time Use Survey, this paper examines which household characteristics are associated with hiring male versus female live-in domestic workers, and how the worker’s gender relates to the allocation of paid and unpaid work within employing households.

Pushed, Not Pulled: The Rise of Own-Account and Domestic Work Among Less-Educated Urban Indian Women

Using the Periodic Labour Force Survey from 2017–18 to 2023–24, this paper examines shifts toward own-account and domestic work among less-educated urban women. It investigates how household and individual characteristics relate to employment patterns and how these relationships have changed over time.
